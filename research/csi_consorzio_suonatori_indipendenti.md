## Overview

C.S.I. (Consorzio Suonatori Indipendenti, "Consortium of Independent Players") is one of the most important Italian alternative rock bands of the 1990s. Formed in 1992 from the ashes of the seminal punk/post-punk band CCCP Fedeli alla linea, C.S.I. expanded the sonic and poetic ambitions of its predecessor into art rock, post-punk, noise rock, and experimental folk territories. The band achieved unexpected mainstream success with their 1997 album *Tabula Rasa Elettrificata*, which reached #1 on the Italian charts, while remaining fiercely independent in ethos. After disbanding in 2002, the remaining members continued as Per Grazia Ricevuta (PGR). A full reunion of the original lineup was announced in January 2026.

## History & Active Years

### Formation (1990–1992)

After CCCP Fedeli alla linea dissolved in 1990 (coinciding with the fall of the Berlin Wall), the core "Emilian nucleus" of Giovanni Lindo Ferretti and Massimo Zamboni joined forces with former members of Litfiba (Gianni Maroccolo, Francesco Magnelli) and their sound engineer Giorgio Canali, along with vocalist Ginevra Di Marco. The name C.S.I. references both "Consorzio Suonatori Indipendenti" and the newly-formed Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS in Italian = CSI), replacing the Soviet-era reference of CCCP.

### Early Period & Breakthrough (1992–1997)

- **1992**: First concert at Centro per l'arte contemporanea Luigi Pecci, Prato (Sep 18), with Üstmamò and Disciplinatha — documented on *Maciste contro tutti*
- **1994**: Debut studio album *Ko de mondo* recorded in Brittany, France; acoustic live *In quiete* for Videomusic
- **1995**: *Materiale resistente* compilation of Resistance songs
- **1996**: *Linea Gotica* — inspired by Balkan war tragedies; cover of Franco Battiato's "E ti vengo a cercare"
- **1997**: *Tabula Rasa Elettrificata* — recorded in Carpineti (Reggio Emilia); reached #1 Italian charts; sold 80,000+ copies

### Later Period & Disbandment (1998–2002)

- **1998**: Final live album *La terra, la guerra, una questione privata* (recorded 1996, released 1998) — concert in memory of writer Beppe Fenoglio; removed from market on May 1, 1998
- **2000**: Massimo Zamboni departs
- **2001**: *Noi non ci saremo* compilation volumes mark the end
- **2002**: Band officially disbands; remaining members form Per Grazia Ricevuta (PGR)

### Post-CSI / Reunions (2013–present)

- **2013**: Ex-CSI tour ("Ciò che non deve accadere accade") with Zamboni, Canali, Maroccolo, Magnelli, Angela Baraldi (replacing Ferretti/Di Marco), Simone Filippi (drums)
- **2013**: *Vicini per chilometri* vinyl box set (Tannen Records/Universal) reissues all 5 official albums on vinyl
- **2015**: Renamed Post-CSI; crowdfunding campaign produces *Breviario partigiano* (book + DVD + CD)
- **2026**: Full original lineup reunion announced (Jan 22); "In viaggio" tour begins August 2026; planned finale in Ulan Bator, Mongolia (2027)

## Status for Demotape Archive

**Assessment**: C.S.I. is a critically important band for the Italian underground/post-punk scene of the 1990s. While they achieved mainstream commercial success (especially with *Tabula Rasa Elettrificata*), they maintained their independent ethos through self-founded labels (I dischi del mulo, CPI) and continued to explore experimental, political, and literary themes. Their connection to CCCP Fedeli alla linea (one of the most influential Italian punk bands) and Litfiba (major 1980s Italian new wave band) makes them a crucial node in the Italian alternative music network.

**Key insights**:
- Direct continuation of CCCP Fedeli alla linea's legacy with expanded musical palette
- Bridge between 1980s Italian punk/post-punk and 1990s indie/alternative rock
- Self-production model through Consorzio Produttori Indipendenti (CPI)
- Connection to Reggio Emilia/Apennine underground scene (Üstmamò, Disciplinatha)
- Major influence on subsequent Italian alternative/indie bands
