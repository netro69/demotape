## Overview

Meathead was an Italian industrial rock band formed in Pordenone (Friuli region) in the late 1980s, with official activity peaking 1992–1997. They were a key part of the Italian underground noise/industrial scene, known for a raw fusion of industrial rock, hip-hop beats, noise, and aggressive sampling. Founded by Mauro "Teho" Teardo (later a renowned film score composer and collaborator with Blixa Bargeld), the band released four albums and several singles/splits before disbanding in the late 1990s. Their work appeared on notable international underground labels including Helter Skelter Records and Sub/Mission Records, and they shared stages and splits with major industrial/noise acts like Cop Shoot Cop, Zeni Geva, Pain Teens, and Babyland.

## History & Active Years

### Formation & Early Period (late 1980s–1992)

Meathead formed in Pordenone (Friuli, northeast Italy) in the late 1980s around the core of Teho Teardo (Mauro Teardo, b. September 19, 1966), Tim Pantado (bass/sampler), Plastic Girl (drums), and Valentine Dunmore (percussion/Atari sampling). The band emerged from the Italian underground noise/industrial scene that also produced Pankow and other Friulian hardcore/industrial acts.

### *Street Knowledge* Era (1992)

Their debut album *Street Knowledge* was released on Helter Skelter Records (1992). Recorded in studios across Europe and the USA, live and by mail, then mixed at Fun in New York City. The album was dedicated to Tim Pantado. It fused industrial rock with hip-hop beats, sampling, and angry underground-culture lyrics. Some tracks were produced by Paolo Favati of Pankow.

### Sub/Mission Records Period (1994–1996)

In 1994, Meathead moved to Sub/Mission Records. They first released the CD Maxi *Dick Smoker* (containing the track "Loser," recorded on 4-tracks), then the full-length *Bored Stuff* (1994) — a 16-track industrial metal CD produced by Paul Ray and Teho Teardo, featuring G.no on bass, Matt on drums, and Guana on sampler. The band also released split singles with Cop Shoot Cop, Zeni Geva, Pain Teens, and Babyland during this period.

In 1996, these split singles were compiled into *Meathead Against The World*, a full-length compilation CD on Sub/Mission Records.

### Final Release & Disbandment (1997)

*Protect Me From What I Want* was released on Dynamica Records in 1997. The band performed "Broken Spine" from this album live on Italian television (TMC2). After this release, Meathead stopped all activities.

## Interesting Facts

1. **International recording**: *Street Knowledge* was recorded in studios across Europe and the USA, live and by mail, then mixed in New York City — unusual for an Italian underground band of the era.

2. **Atari sampling**: Valentine Dunmore used an Atari computer for sampling, giving the band a distinctive lo-fi digital texture.

3. **Teho Teardo's post-Meatard career**: After Meathead, Teardo became one of Italy's most respected film score composers, working with Paolo Sorrentino (*Il Divo*, *The Family Friend*), Gabriele Salvatores (*Denti*), and Andrea Molaioli (*Slam*).

4. **Blixa Bargeld connection**: Teardo and Blixa Bargeld met at a Swans concert in Berlin in 1988 and later collaborated on multiple critically acclaimed albums (*Still Smiling*, *Nerissimo*, *Christian & Mauro*).

5. **Sub/Mission Records**: The US-based Sub/Mission Records was a key industrial/noise label that also released material by Cop Shoot Cop and other international noise rock acts, giving Meathead significant underground credibility.

6. **Pankow production**: Paolo Favati of Pankow (one of Italy's most important hardcore/industrial bands) produced some Meathead tracks, linking the Friulian and Emilian underground scenes.
