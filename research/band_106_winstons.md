## 1. The American Winstons (1960s, Washington D.C.)

### Overview

The Winstons were an American funk and soul group formed circa **1960** in **Washington, D.C.**, led by guitarist/vocalist/saxophonist **Richard Lewis Spencer** (born 1942, died December 2020 at age 78). They were notable as a **racially integrated** band during the civil rights era. The group achieved mainstream success with their 1969 single "Color Him Father" b/w "Amen, Brother" and are now recognized as one of the most influential acts in modern music history due to the "Amen Break" — the most sampled drum break in recorded music.

**Active Years:** 1960–1970 (original run); revived 1996 by Joe Phillips (no original members)
**Genres:** Soul, Funk, R&B
**Labels:** Metromedia, Curtom (Curtis Mayfield's label), Sensational Records (later reissues)

### Connections & Influence (American Winstons)

#### Direct Musical Connections
- **Curtis Mayfield / The Impressions:** The Winstons were discovered by Curtis Mayfield's group while playing in a D.C. club. They became the **touring backup band for The Impressions**. Mayfield's Curtom label released their first single. "Amen, Brother" was based on a guitar riff Mayfield played for Spencer and samples "Theme from Lilies of the Field (Amen)" by The Impressions.
- **Otis Redding:** Members G.C. Coleman and Quincy Mattison were veterans of **Otis Redding's band** before relocating to Washington D.C.
- **Motown Sessions:** G.C. Coleman worked as a **Motown session drummer**.
- **Richard Spencer** later performed with **Leroy Taylor & The Four Keys**.
- **G.C. Coleman** later played with rock band **Hillow Hammet** and **The Georgia Power Band**.

#### The Amen Break — Cultural Influence

The 6-7 second drum solo by G.C. Coleman in "Amen, Brother" (starting at ~1:26) has become **the most sampled drum break in music history**, used over 4,500+ times across genres:

- **Hip-Hop:** N.W.A ("Straight Outta Compton"), Dr. Dre, Slick Rick, Ice Cube
- **Drum & Bass / Jungle:** LTJ Bukem, Goldie, Aphrodite, 4Hero, Photek
- **Rock:** Oasis, Nine Inch Nails (indirect influence via sampling culture)
- **Electronic:** Countless breakbeat and jungle tracks
- **Advertising & TV:** Used in countless commercials, TV shows, films
- **Video Games:** Referenced in various game soundtracks

**Notable fact:** Neither Spencer nor Coleman ever received royalties from the Amen Break's use. Spencer was unaware of its sampling until 1996 (after the statute of limitations had passed). Coleman died homeless in Atlanta in 2006. In 2015, UK DJ Martyn Webster set up a GoFundMe for Spencer, raising ~£15,000.

### Notable Facts

1. 🏆 **Grammy Winner:** "Color Him Father" won the **1970 Grammy Award for Best R&B Song** — a tribute to a stepfather raising his wife's children.
2. 📊 **Chart Success:** "Color Him Father" reached **Top 10** on both R&B and Pop charts; sold over one million copies.
3. 🥁 **Most Sampled Track:** "Amen, Brother" (specifically the Amen Break) is widely considered the **most sampled single track in music history** — more than "Funky Drummer" (James Brown) or "Apache" (Incredible Bongo Band).
4. ⚖️ **Royalty Controversy:** The Winstons never received royalties for the Amen Break; Spencer called its sampling "plagiarism" but later said it was "flattering."
5. 🔄 **Racial Integration:** The Winstons were a rare **racially integrated** soul band in the mid-1960s.
6. 📖 **Post-Band Career:** Spencer retired from the Washington Metropolitan Transit Authority after 30 years and published a novel; Coleman died in poverty in Atlanta.
7. 🔁 **Revival:** Joe Phillips owns The Winstons trademark and has kept "The Winstons Orchestra" touring since 1996 (no original members).
