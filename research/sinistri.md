## TL;DR

Sinistri is the continuation of **Starfuckers**, one of Italy's most important avant-garde/experimental rock bands of the late 1980s–2000s. Originally formed as Starfuckers in 1987 by five musicians from Lunigiana (Tuscany) who adopted Bologna as their base, the group evolved through several phases: from raw Stooges-inspired garage/noise-rock → musique concrète-influenced "concrete rock" → free-jazz/cubist post-rock → dark electronic/intuitive music trio as Sinistri (2000–2004). They reverted to the Starfuckers name in 2008. Their work is heavily cited as foundational to the Italian experimental scene. The group is known for radical exploration of non-metric rhythms, asynchrony, and "intuitive music."

### Brief History

Starfuckers formed in 1987 when five young men from the Lunigiana area of Tuscany came together with a raw, minimal, high-power approach to rock. Their debut album *Metallic Diseases* (1989) was compared to Royal Trux's retro-brainy garage-blues and the post-Stooges noise of the New York no-wave scene. Over the next decade they underwent one of the most radical evolutions in Italian music: from noisy garage punk → abstract musique concrète-influenced "concrete rock" (cited alongside This Heat, Faust, Oval) → cryptic post-rock (*Infrantumi*, 1997) → sampledelic/funk-jazz/noise fusion (*Infinitive Sessions*, 2002).

In ~2000 the group (now a trio with Dino Bramanti added as electronic technician) renamed themselves **Sinistri**, taking the name from their own 1994 album. As Sinistri they explored intuitive music, non-metric rhythms, and dark electronic textures, releasing *Free Pulse* (2005) and *Timing The 183K Pulse* (2006). They reverted to the Starfuckers name in 2008.

Frontman **Manuele Giannini** continues to be active with projects including **Sinistri++**, **Weight & Treble** (with Massimo Carozzi), and solo work.

## 4. Notable Facts

### Musical Evolution & Philosophy

1. **"Concrete Rock"**: Starfuckers described their own music as "concrete rock" — self-described musique concrète played with rock instrumentation. They are considered one of the most important acts in Italian experimental music.

2. **Radical Mutation**: Piero Scaruffi's History of Rock noted Starfuckers "staged one of the most impressive mutations in memory" — evolving from garage punk to abstract post-rock to sampledelic noise within 15 years.

3. **Philosophy of Time**: Sinistri's manifesto states: "The essence of music is time, not sound... it is not made of multiple sounds distributed in uniform time, but of a single sound bouncing in multiple time." (from OndaRock review of *Free Pulse*)

4. **Rejection of Metric Rhythm**: The band's core exploration is non-metric, aperiodic, asynchronous musical structures — what they call "intuitive music."

5. **The Sinistri Name**: The name "Sinistri" was taken directly from their 1994 album *Sinistri*. It means "left-hand" or "ominous" in Italian — a deliberate choice to signal both a new phase and continuity with their past.

6. **Live Resurrection**: After years of inactivity, Starfuckers played a notable comeback concert on **April 30, 2010** at Locomotiv in Bologna, confirming ongoing creative life.

7. **Nonlinear Narrative TV Project**: In 2014, Starfuckers created "The Cabinet-Maker," described as a "nonlinear television series" — an opening theme for a TV series that was never actually produced.

8. **Controversy**: *Infinitive Sessions* (2002) was heavily sampled by **Matmos** on their album *The Civil War* (2003) without acknowledgment or compensation — noted on the Bandcamp page.

9. **Dino Bramanti**: Joined the group as electronic/digital technician, enabling the transition to Sinistri's more electronic sound. He is credited for "real-time processing/live mixing" on *Free Pulse*.

10. **Reissues**: *Metallic Diseases* was reissued on LP by Holy Mountain / Tlön Uqbar in 2011, including bonus track "Grado Zero" and a download card. *Infrantumi* was reissued on LP by The New Noise in 2017.
